Sliding Detectors

How the network reads a curve: not by looking at it all at once, but by dragging small pattern-matchers along it.

One detector, dragged along the curve

A detector is a small fixed pattern — five numbers. It is laid over five neighbouring points of your curve, and it reports how well the curve matches its pattern there. Then it shifts one point right and reports again. Sixty points, sixty reports.

Why sliding, rather than looking at fixed places

This is the part that matters for your data. Your measurement windows are cropped at random — the generator deliberately crops up to 2.5 decades — so the same physical feature lands in a different place on every curve.

Three passes, each building on the last

It does not happen once — it happens three times over, and each pass works on the output of the pass before rather than on your curve directly.

What comes out of step 4 128 detectors, each having reported a score at all 60 positions. That is 128 × 60 numbers — far too many to carry forward, which is exactly the problem step 5 exists to solve, and why it solves it two ways at once.
